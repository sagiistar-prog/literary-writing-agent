# Writing Framework

This framework defines the creative dimensions the Agent can help organize. It is intentionally high level and does not imitate any living author's style.

## 1. Story Premise

A compact statement of the story situation: who is under pressure, what has changed, and why it matters now.

## 2. Theme

The emotional or philosophical question the story keeps returning to. A theme should create pressure, not function as a slogan.

## 3. Protagonist Desire

The visible thing the protagonist wants and the deeper need beneath it. The Agent should distinguish between stated desire and emotional hunger.

## 4. Conflict

Conflict can come from people, institutions, landscape, memory, social expectation, secrecy, or a contradiction inside the protagonist.

## 5. World Texture

The sensory, social, material, and seasonal details that make the fictional world feel lived in. World texture should serve mood and choice.

## 6. Chapter Arc

Each chapter should change the reader's knowledge, the character's options, or the emotional pressure. A chapter arc does not need spectacle, but it needs movement.

## 7. Scene Purpose

Before revising a scene, identify what it must accomplish: reveal a desire, expose a contradiction, shift power, plant a motif, or force a choice.

## 8. Sensory Details

Sensory details should be selective. They can include sound, temperature, texture, smell, light, distance, and bodily perception when relevant to the character's experience.

## 9. Emotional Subtext

Subtext is what the scene feels without stating directly. The Agent should look for silence, avoidance, misdirection, repeated gestures, and objects that carry pressure.

## 10. Revision Notes

Revision notes should explain:

- what changed
- why it changed
- which craft dimension was strengthened
- what risk still needs manual review
